My research focuses on statistical and computational approaches for understanding biological aging, dementia, age-related disease, and individual variation in health outcomes.

## Biological Aging

I investigate biological aging using molecular and clinical aging measures, including multi-omics aging signatures, proteomic and metabolomic profiles, epigenetic aging clocks, frailty-related phenotypes, and other biomarkers of physiological aging.

A major objective is to determine whether biological aging measures provide clinically relevant information beyond chronological age.

## Dementia and Precision Risk Prediction

I develop and evaluate statistical and machine-learning approaches for prediction of Alzheimer's disease and related dementias, cognitive decline, mortality, and other aging-related outcomes.

This work integrates demographic, clinical, genetic, molecular, and longitudinal information to improve personalized risk assessment.

## Statistical Genetics

My research includes genome-wide association analyses, polygenic risk prediction, genetic epidemiology, and integration of genetic susceptibility with clinical and molecular biomarkers.

## Multi-Omics

I work with genomic, epigenomic, proteomic, metabolomic, and clinical biomarker data from large population-based studies (e.g., UK Biobank and Swedish Twin Registry).

A central methodological interest is integrating heterogeneous biological data using statistical learning and machine-learning approaches.

## Statistical and Machine-Learning Methods

My methodological work includes:

- Survival and time-to-event modelling
- Longitudinal analysis
- Machine learning and ensemble learning
- Bayesian modelling
- Causal inference
- High-dimensional prediction
- Polygenic risk modelling
- Multi-omics integration
- Epidemiological study design and analysis

## Population Resources

My research uses large-scale population cohorts, Swedish health and population registers, twin studies, dementia registries, and international biomedical resources.
