## Bioinformatician & Biostatistician

I am a Bioinformatician at the Department of Medical Epidemiology and Biostatistics at Karolinska Institutet in Stockholm, Sweden. My work lies at the intersection of biostatistics, bioinformatics, statistical genetics, machine learning, and epidemiology.

My research focuses on developing and applying statistical and computational methods to understand biological aging, dementia, and age-related disease, and to improve individualized risk prediction. I work with large-scale population cohorts, health registers, genetic data, proteomics, metabolomics, clinical biomarkers, and other high-dimensional biomedical data.

My methodological interests include statistical genetics, multi-omics integration, biological aging measures and aging clocks, survival and longitudinal modelling, causal inference, and machine-learning approaches for precision health.

## Research Interests

- Biological aging and aging clocks
- Dementia and Alzheimer's disease and related dementias
- Statistical genetics and polygenic risk prediction
- Multi-omics integration
- Machine learning and artificial intelligence in health
- Survival and risk modelling
- Longitudinal data analysis
- Causal inference
- Population-based and registry research

## Current Research

My current research integrates genetic, molecular, clinical, and epidemiological information to improve the characterization and prediction of aging-related health outcomes.

Major areas include:

- Multi-omics prediction of dementia and age-related disease
- Biological aging and mortality prediction
- Proteomic and metabolomic aging signatures
- Statistical and machine-learning approaches for risk prediction
- Genetic and molecular epidemiology of aging
